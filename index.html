03 Notes

What actually made this readable: the first host was reachable directly over the VPN, so everything up to the foothold was plain Kali traffic. Only the second network needed a pivot — which is why chisel appears exactly once, between DB26 and DC20.
Two things cost me the most time and both were my own tooling, not the target: a wrong AES string-to-key convention (wrongly assumed UTF-16LE, retried several times before calibrating against a known password/hash pair), and a gMSA password blob that decodes to garbage in this lab by design. Neither had anything to do with the boxes.
Scope: this is an intentionally vulnerable training lab, assessed under its rules of engagement. Passwords, hashes and flags above belong to one instance — rotate everything if you reuse the range.
